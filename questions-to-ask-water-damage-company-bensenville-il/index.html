Questions to Ask a Water Damage Company in Bensenville

Wet floors rush people into signing whatever is put in front of them, so decide your questions before the first call.

By the Bensenville Water Damage Pros team

(630) 580-6401

The best questions to ask a water damage company are the ones that force specifics, such as what will be done, who will do it, how drying will be proven and how the price is built. A stressed homeowner or renter standing in an inch of water is easy to rush, and a few plain questions slow things down in a useful way. Keep this list on your phone and read from it during the call.

Ask for the scope in writing

A written scope lists the rooms, the materials to be removed or dried, the equipment and what is excluded. Ask for it before work starts, not after. A company that explains scope and price before work starts is making the job easy to compare, and one that avoids writing it down is telling you something.

Ask what changes would alter the price, such as finding wet insulation behind a wall, and how you will be told before extra work happens. Surprises are normal in water jobs, but silent surprises are not.

Ask about the S500 standard

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the standard for professional water damage restoration. You are allowed to ask any company whether its crews work to S500, and what that means for how they classify the water, set up drying and decide what to remove.

Do not accept a vague answer such as we do it the right way. Ask them to explain how the water category and class are decided on your job. A good answer names the water source, how far it spread and what materials are involved.

Ask who actually does the work

Some companies employ their own crews and some use subcontractors for part of a job. Neither is wrong, but you should know which you have. This site is a dispatch line that sends licensed and insured restoration professionals from several contractors, so the useful question to ask any company, including this one, is whether the company or a subcontractor will perform the work.

Follow with a few more. Who will be on site, who will you reach if something goes wrong, and who is responsible for the work after the crew leaves? Ask to see proof of licensing and insurance for whoever shows up.

Ask for moisture logs and documentation

Drying is measured, not guessed. Ask whether the crew will record moisture readings in the affected materials on arrival and at each visit, and whether you can see them. The final readings tell you whether the material reached a dry standard before anyone closed a wall.

Ask for photos of the affected areas, the equipment placed and the materials removed. Those records are what your insurer may ask to see, and a company that offers them without prompting is thinking ahead.

Bensenville details to check before you hire

Bensenville's Community and Economic Development office handles building inspections and lists permits for work such as water heater replacement, additions and remodels, and sanitary sewer replacement. For other repairs, the Village says to ask whether a permit applies, and you can reach scheduling at 630-350-3413. Ask any company whether it will check on permits for your job, and do not assume the answer.

Census QuickFacts shows about half of homes in the village are owner-occupied, so many callers are renters. If you rent, ask your landlord who may hire the company and who signs the scope. Nearby, households in Addison and Franklin Park are served by the same dispatch line.

Questions about timing and what happens next

Ask what the company can do the same day when available, and be wary of anyone who promises an exact arrival time before they know your address and the situation. A fair answer describes what happens first, such as stopping the source, extracting standing water and setting up drying equipment.

Then ask about the end of the job. How will you know drying is complete, what will they leave behind, and what must you arrange separately, such as rebuilding drywall or flooring? A clear handoff keeps you from paying twice for the same step.

Pricing, payment and insurer paperwork

Ask how the price is explained. Honest pricing follows factors such as the amount of water, the materials affected, the equipment days and the removal and rebuild needed, without exact numbers over the phone before anyone has seen the job. Insist on seeing the figure before work starts.

Be cautious if a company demands full payment before starting. Ask for payment terms in writing, and for what happens if the scope changes. For help with your claim, ask what documents the company will provide, and do not accept a promise that your insurer will pay, because only the insurer decides.

For jobs that begin with standing water, ask how water extraction will be followed by structural drying and dehumidification, and how each stage is recorded.

Questions about this topic

What is the single most important question to ask?

Ask for the scope and price in writing before work starts. It forces the company to be specific and gives you something to compare.

If a company will not write it down, move to the next one.

Does the company that answers the phone do the work itself?

Not always. Some networks, including this dispatch line, send licensed and insured professionals from several contractors.

Ask whether the company or a subcontractor will perform the work and who to call if you have a problem.

Should I pay the full amount before work begins?

Be careful with that request. Ask for payment terms in writing and understand what is due when.

Pressure to pay everything up front before a scope is written is a reason to pause.

What if my insurer wants documents from the company?

Ask the company what it provides, such as photos, moisture logs, an itemized scope and invoices.

Your insurer decides coverage, so ask the insurer what it needs, and report damage as soon as you decide to file.

Do I need a permit for water damage repair in Bensenville?

It depends on the work. The Village lists permits for items like water heater replacement and sanitary sewer replacement.

For other repairs, ask the Village Community and Economic Development office whether a permit applies.
