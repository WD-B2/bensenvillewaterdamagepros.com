Water Damage Restoration in Downtown Bensenville, IL

Condo buildings, townhomes and older houses sit close to the Metra line and Center Street. Here is how water damage in a shared building is usually handled.

(630) 580-6401

Downtown Bensenville is the village's own label for the area around the Metra station and Towne Center Park. The 2015 Village plan calls it the Downtown District. This page is for homes, condos and small buildings in and around that district, not for the whole village. For village-wide service, start at the Bensenville home page.

Housing near downtown is a mix. Real estate directories list condominium buildings and townhome developments, including a Town Center condo building, next to older single-family blocks. In a building with several units, one failed supply line can reach a neighbor's ceiling, so the first call often involves more than one household.

Where this page applies

The Metra Milwaukee District West station sits on West Main Street, and Village Hall is at 12 S Center Street. We use those landmarks because the village has not published a neighborhood boundary, and we do not claim one here. If your building is a few blocks outside that core, the same advice still applies.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. The village names Willow Creek, Silver Creek and Addison Creek as its three flood sources, and its Engineering Division at (630) 350-3435 can tell you whether a specific address sits in a mapped floodplain. Most downtown water calls start inside the building anyway, with a pipe, a fixture or an appliance.

When water is moving through a downtown unit

Work through these four moves in order, then call (630) 580-6401 and say which floor you are on, where the water started and whether neighbors are affected.

Find the right shut-off

Try the fixture valve first, then the unit shut-off under a sink or behind a washer. In a condo or apartment the main may be shared, so call the building manager or association if you cannot stop it.

Power in wet rooms

Switch off breakers for wet rooms only if you can reach the panel on dry floor. If water is near outlets or the panel, stay away and call 911 or the electric utility.

Tell the unit below

Knock on the neighbor's door and let them know. Water often shows up first as a stain or drip in the ceiling below, and early notice limits what gets soaked.

Photograph, then lift

Take photos of the source and the wet areas before you move anything. Then lift small items, rugs and electronics off wet floors and keep children and pets out of water from a drain or toilet.

Condos, townhomes and older houses lose water in different ways

In a condo or townhome, the common causes are a appliance supply line or drain, a leaking tub or shower, a failed kitchen fixture and water coming down from the unit above. Shared walls and floors mean the wet area often crosses a unit line.

In an older single-family house, water usually starts at a supply line, a water heater or a roof or window leak. A ceiling that sags or stains needs attention fast, because wet drywall and insulation hold water long after the source is closed. Wet wall and drywall sections are opened, dried and rebuilt only after readings show they are dry.

Who handles what in a shared building

Condo associations often carry a master policy while unit owners carry their own, and which one responds depends on the governing documents and the cause of the loss. Renters usually need to tell the landlord first. A restoration crew can dry what is wet and describe the work in writing, but it cannot decide coverage.

Before any equipment goes in, a crew in a multi-unit building will usually ask who can approve access to the unit below and where the building shut-offs and electrical rooms are. Having the manager's number and the association rules ready speeds that up.

Basements and sewer lines near downtown

If your building has a basement or a lower level, a floor drain backup or a sump failure is a different job from a supply-line leak. Sewage-related water is treated as contaminated, and it calls for sewage backup cleanup, not household fans or an ordinary vacuum. Standing water is removed first, then affected materials are assessed. See basement water damage for the full sequence.

The Village runs an Overhead Sewer Program for homeowners in incorporated Bensenville who install an overhead sewer or a backflow prevention device. It requires permits and approval before work starts, so call Village Hall at 630-766-8200 before you hire anyone. The 2015 village plan also listed sewer replacement for the 200 block of South Center Street among planned projects. This page does not confirm whether that work is finished.

Extraction and drying in a building with neighbors

The first step is water extraction, pulling standing water out with pumps and extraction units. Next comes structural drying: moisture meters and thermal imaging map the wet areas, air movers push air across surfaces and dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room. Readings are taken again each visit until the materials reach their dry standard.

Drying equipment is loud and draws power, which matters in a building where neighbors share walls and electrical service. A good plan tells the people next door what to expect and for how many days. Not every wet material can be saved, and a crew that says otherwise before checking readings is guessing.

Keep records for your insurer or your association

Photos of the source and every wet room, a note of when you noticed the water, receipts for emergency repairs and the moisture readings from the drying crew all help describe the loss. An itemized scope of the work can help too. Whether any of it is covered depends on the cause of damage and the policy terms, so check your policy and talk to your insurer or association.

Questions about Downtown Bensenville

What area does this page cover?

Homes, condos and small buildings in and around the Downtown District near the Metra station and Towne Center Park. The village has not published a formal neighborhood boundary, so we describe the area by landmarks.

What if the water came from the unit above mine?

Photograph the damage, tell your landlord or association, and say so when you call. The source unit has to be fixed before drying can finish, and who pays depends on the building documents and each policy.

Does the condo association or my own policy cover water damage?

It depends on the association documents, the cause of the loss and the policy wording. Ask your insurer and your association which one responds before you agree to repairs.

Can the village help with sewer backup protection?

The Village has an Overhead Sewer Program for homeowners in incorporated Bensenville. Permits and approval are required before work, so call Village Hall at 630-766-8200 for current terms.

Is emergency water removal available near downtown?

The phone line is answered around the clock, and a crew is sent when one is available for your address. Describe what you see and what you have already shut off.
