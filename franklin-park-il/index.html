Franklin Park is a Cook County village of 18,467 people at the 2020 Census, with ZIP code 60131. This page covers how its older housing, combined sewers and flood programs affect the calls you make.

Older houses, older pipes in Franklin Park

The Cook County hazard mitigation plan annex says most Franklin Park housing and industrial and commercial buildings date from the 1940s to the 1960s. Buildings from that period can have aging supply lines, original drain stacks and basement floor drains tied to the village sewer. If your house or plant is in that group, inspect washing machine hoses, water heater connections and floor drains for corrosion or damp stains.

Age alone does not mean a leak is coming. It does mean a slow drip behind a wall can run for months before a stain shows. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera help crews find that hidden wet material.

When sewer water or a pipe floods your Franklin Park basement

A basement that fills from a drain or a broken pipe needs quick decisions made safely. Run through these four, then call (630) 580-6401 at any hour.

Stop adding water

Do not flush toilets, run the dishwasher or start laundry if a drain is backing up. For a broken pipe, shut the fixture valve or the main, usually near the meter.

Stay off wet floors

Do not walk into a flooded basement to reach the panel, the furnace or the water heater. Call 911 or the utility if you suspect an electric or gas hazard.

Protect the upper floors

Carry valuables and papers upstairs, and prop furniture legs on foil or blocks if you cannot move them. Leave carpet and heavy items for the crew.

Count it as dirty

Water that came up a drain mixes sewage with stormwater. Keep family and pets away, and photograph the room and waterline before pumping starts.

Combined sewers and what they mean for backups

The village says most of Franklin Park sits in a combined sewer area, where sanitary sewage and stormwater drain into the same pipes and flow to the Metropolitan Water Reclamation District for treatment. The Cook County annex also records urban flooding from storm sewer and combined sewer backups during major storms, and ranks repetitive urban flood losses as the community's top ongoing concern.

In practice, a heavy rain can load the pipes that also serve your toilets and floor drains. That is why an overhead sewer or a properly maintained backflow device matters in this village, and why a basement backup should be treated as contaminated. Our sewage backup cleanup service covers extraction and disinfection.

The village cost share programs and permits

Franklin Park runs two Drainage and Sewer Cost Share Programs, one for rear yard drainage and one for overhead sewer conversion. The village reimburses up to 50 percent of eligible costs for work that reduces how often or how severely private property floods. The Engineering Department must approve the application, and a building permit must be in hand before work is done.

For structural flooding, basement seepage or standing water that will not drain, the Engineering Department at (847) 671-8304 offers a drainage review. The Building Department at 9500 Belmont is at (847) 671-8245. Complete repairs only after you ask about permits and your insurer's rules.

Creeks, buyouts and flood storage nearby

The Des Plaines River forms an eastern boundary of the village, and the Crystal Creek and Silver Creek tributaries have flooded dozens of properties. The annex reports Silver Creek channel improvements that reduced the flood hazard for 92 homes, local flood storage totaling 100 acre-feet built in 2017, and a plan to acquire 32 flood-prone homes along Silver Creek.

FEMA identified 34 repetitive loss properties in the village. Buyers and tenants should ask about prior water events and look up the FEMA flood map. The annex also says the flood maps are considered inadequate, so ask the village what it holds for your address.

Drying, standards and the water supply

Franklin Park's drinking water is bought from the City of Chicago and comes from Lake Michigan, so a burst supply pipe usually means clean water at first. It turns into a bigger job if it sits in carpet pad or drywall, so crews dry with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, logging readings until the materials are dry.

Ask any company whether its crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500, the water damage restoration standard. If mold shows up, S520 is the remediation standard, and health questions belong with a physician.

Questions about Franklin Park

Do you cover every ZIP code in Franklin Park?

Franklin Park uses ZIP code 60131, the code on the village offices at 9500 Belmont Avenue. We cover it from the Bensenville edge on the west to the Des Plaines River side on the east, including the Mannheim Road and Belmont Avenue corridors. If your mailing address differs, call and we will confirm.

Is emergency water removal available in Franklin Park?

Yes. Emergency dispatch runs 24/7 with same-day response when available. We do not promise an arrival time, and we will tell you how to stay safe while you wait.

Will the village pay for an overhead sewer?

The Overhead Sewer Cost Share Program reimburses up to 50 percent of eligible costs. Engineering must approve your application and a building permit is required before work. Call (847) 671-8304 for details.

Why does my Franklin Park basement back up in heavy rain?

The village says most of it is a combined sewer area, so sewage and stormwater share pipes and can overload in major storms. A drainage review from the Engineering Department can help assess your property.

Is a sewer backup covered by insurance?

Coverage for sewer backup depends on your policy and often needs an added endorsement. Flood is usually excluded. Read the policy wording and ask your insurer before assuming anything.

Do you work on factories and warehouses in Franklin Park?

Yes. The same 24/7 line sends crews to commercial and industrial buildings, with drying planned around access and production.
