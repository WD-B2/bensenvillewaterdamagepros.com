Where Addison water calls start, from Army Trail Road to Salt Creek Park

Addison is a big village, about 10 square miles, and calls come from very different settings. Army Trail Road, built in 1832 over an older trail, cuts across it, and the village's industrial district adds warehouses and plants to the housing. A roof drain failure in a warehouse and a washing machine hose in a ranch house need different tools, so the first question we ask is what kind of building it is.

Homes and businesses near any creek corridor should look up their flood status before an emergency, not during one. When a pipe, sump or sewer line fails in Addison

A failing pipe, sump pit or sewer line can put an inch of water across a floor in a few minutes. Do these four things, then call (630) 580-6401 whatever the hour.

Stop the flow

Shut the supply valve under the fixture, or the main valve where the water line enters the house. If the leak is in the street, call the village and describe where the water is surfacing.

Cut power with care

Turn off breakers feeding wet rooms only when you can stand on a dry surface to do it. Standing water near a panel means leave the house and call 911 or the electric utility.

Move things up

Put electronics, paperwork, shoes and boxes on tables and counters. Pull up small rugs so they do not stain the floor, and leave sofas and beds for the crew.

Avoid dirty water

Sewage or sink backup water carries bacteria, so keep children and pets out and wash hands after contact. Take photos of the room and the water line before cleanup starts.

Sewer backups and the lateral in Addison

Addison tells homeowners that the 6-inch lateral connecting a house to the village sewer is theirs up to the inside edge of the public sidewalk. Common causes of backups are flushed paper products, grease and tree roots entering the pipe. If a village-owned tree between the sidewalk and street is the cause, the village will clear the blockage.

For a sanitary sewer backup, the village lists 630/279-2140, and after hours it points to the police non-emergency line at 630/543-3080. If only one drain backs up while others run normally, that drain is likely blocked and the main line is probably fine. When sewage has reached finished floors, our sewage backup cleanup service handles extraction and disinfection.

The village also notes that some homes have a backflow prevention device on the lateral, which should be inspected every year, and it names an overhead sewer system as the most reliable protection. Ask a licensed plumber what fits your house.

Floodplain rules and the Salt Creek gage

Addison treats floodplain and floodway areas as special management areas that require special permits, and it checks stormwater plans against both village and DuPage County ordinances. Streams and ponds exist inside the village, so a property near one should confirm its status with the village engineering staff at (630) 693-7530.

USGS lists a station called Salt Creek at Addison, number 05531200, with a drainage area of 83.7 square miles. Its peak-flow record runs from 1948 to 1976, so it shows the creek's long history but is not a live gauge today. Flood water is excluded from most standard policies, so ask your insurer about flood coverage.

Owners, renters and landlords in Addison

The Census reports 67.1 percent of Addison homes are owner-occupied, which leaves about a third rented. Permits and drying standards in Addison
