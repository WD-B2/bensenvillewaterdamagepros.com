Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage? Bensenville Guide

A plain answer for Bensenville homeowners and renters on which water losses a standard policy tends to cover, and which it tends to leave out.

By the Bensenville Water Damage Pros team

(630) 580-6401

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage? Often yes, but only for certain kinds of water loss. A standard homeowners policy is a contract that lists what it pays for and what it excludes, and water is one of the most argued-over items on that list.

The short version is that sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while slow leaks, neglect and flooding often are not. This guide explains the dividing lines in general terms. Your own policy wording decides the real answer.

The sudden and accidental test

Most policies draw one main line. Water that escapes suddenly and accidentally, such as a supply line that splits behind a vanity or a pipe that bursts in a wall, is commonly treated as a covered event. The word that matters is sudden. You walk in and find it, and you can usually point to a day it happened.

Policies also tend to split the loss into pieces. There is the damage to the structure, the damage to belongings, and sometimes a separate item for the cost of tearing out part of a wall to reach the broken pipe. Each piece can have its own limit or its own condition, so read all of them.

What is usually covered and what usually is not

The table below is a general pattern, not a promise. Wording differs from policy to policy, and endorsements can add or remove protection. Treat it as a list of questions to ask your insurer.

Neighbors in Wood Dale and Addison deal with the same policy language, so a question that came up for them is likely to apply to you too.

SituationOften coveredOften excluded or limited
Supply line or pipe bursts suddenlyYes, as a sudden and accidental lossThe failed pipe itself may be limited
Water heater tank fails and floods a roomResulting water damage is commonly coveredThe heater itself is usually a maintenance item
Slow drip under a sink for monthsRarelyGradual leakage and neglect are common exclusions
Rain or river water entering the basementRarelyFlood is usually excluded without separate flood coverage
Sewer or drain backupOnly with an added endorsement in many policiesOften excluded in the base policy
Roof damaged by wind, then rain gets inOften, when the opening came from a covered eventWear and age of the roof are usually excluded

Why gradual leaks get denied

A leak that has been dripping for weeks leaves rot, staining or mold, and insurers tend to read that as a maintenance problem rather than an accident. The same logic applies to a shower that seeped into a subfloor over a year. Many policies exclude repeated seepage over time.

This is why dating the damage matters. If you cannot say when it began, the insurer may assume it was gradual. Photos taken the day you find the problem, and a plumber's note about the failed part, help show what kind of event it was.

Flood is a separate question

Water that comes in from outside the house is handled differently from water that comes from a pipe. Heavy rain overwhelming the yard, a creek leaving its banks or groundwater pushing through a basement slab all fall under flood in most policies. The Illinois Department of Insurance notes that most policies do not cover floods unless flood coverage was bought separately.

That means a basement that fills after a storm can be a very different claim from one that fills because a burst pipe let go. Both need drying, but only one may be a covered loss. If you are unsure which applies, ask your insurer before assuming. Storm-driven cases are covered in more detail on the flood damage cleanup page.

Local facts that affect a Bensenville claim

About half of Bensenville homes are rentals. Census QuickFacts puts owner-occupancy at 50.8 percent across 6,834 households. In practice that means two different policies often sit under one roof: the landlord's policy on the building and the tenant's renters policy on belongings. If you rent, ask which of them applies before you start throwing anything out.

The Village states that it cannot prevent flooding, and it identifies three flood sources with their own floodplains: Willow Creek, Silver Creek (also called Bensenville Ditch) and Addison Creek. The Village Engineering Division at (630) 350-3435 can help you learn whether a property sits in a floodplain, and FEMA's map service shows the same. Knowing that before a storm tells you whether a separate flood policy is worth pricing.

Steps that protect a claim

Illinois insurance guidance is practical here. Photograph or video the damaged areas and belongings, keep receipts for emergency repairs, and make essential temporary repairs to stop further damage. Hold off on permanent repairs until you have spoken to the insurer, because it may not pay for repairs it did not authorize.

Stopping the water is not optional. Shutting the supply, pulling out soaked rugs and starting water extraction are the kind of temporary steps a policy usually expects. Keep the paperwork for all of it.

Questions worth asking your insurer

Write the answers down with the date and the name of the person you spoke with. A phone call you cannot reconstruct later is not much use in a dispute.

  • Does my policy cover sudden pipe breaks, and what counts as sudden?
  • Is there a limit on tear-out and access costs?
  • Do I have sewer or drain backup coverage, and what is its limit?
  • Is flood excluded, and is separate flood coverage available?
  • What is my deductible for water losses, and does it differ by cause?
  • How long do I have to report, and how long do I have to recover depreciation?

If the claim is denied or the loss is small

If a claim is denied, ask for the denial in writing. The Illinois Department of Insurance says the next step is to contact the state insurance department. Small losses are a separate judgment call, since a claim may sit against your record and your deductible may exceed the repair.

Cleanup should not wait on that decision. A wet wall keeps getting worse whether or not anyone has decided who pays. Services such as structural drying stop the spread while the paperwork sorts itself out.

Questions about this topic

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage from a burst pipe?

Often yes, when the break was sudden and accidental. Policies commonly cover the resulting damage to floors, walls and belongings, though the pipe itself may be treated differently.

Your deductible and any tear-out limit still apply. Read the water section of your policy or ask your insurer directly.

Is a leaking roof covered?

It depends on the cause. Storm damage that opens the roof is often treated as covered, while a roof that leaks from age or wear usually is not. Photos of the opening and the date of the storm help.

Does homeowners insurance cover flooding in the basement?

Usually not. Flood damage is generally excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Sewer or drain backup may be covered only if you added that endorsement. Ask your insurer which category your loss falls into.

I rent in Bensenville. Whose insurance pays for water damage?

With roughly half of Bensenville households renting, this comes up often. The landlord's policy generally relates to the building, and your renters policy relates to your belongings and sometimes extra living costs.

Report the problem to your landlord in writing, photograph your belongings, and ask your own insurer what your renters policy includes.

Should I pay for repairs before the insurer visits?

Make essential temporary repairs to prevent further damage, and keep receipts. Wait on permanent repairs until you have talked to the insurer. A reasonable wait for an adjuster on a minor claim is about 3 to 5 days, according to Illinois guidance.
