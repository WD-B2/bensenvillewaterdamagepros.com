Water damage restoration in Wood Dale means stopping the water, pulling out what is standing, and drying the structure before wet drywall and subfloor start to break down. Our line takes emergency calls 24/7 and sends licensed and insured restoration professionals from Bensenville, which sits just east of the city.

Wood Dale is a DuPage County city of 14,012 people at the 2020 Census, and 60191 is its ZIP code. What follows explains how the city's water, sewer and creek systems shape your first calls after a leak.

Who runs the water and sewer in Wood Dale

Wood Dale is listed by the DuPage Water Commission as a customer, the City of Wood Dale. Inside the city, the Utilities Division of Public Works operates the drinking water and storm sewer systems. Public Works is at 790 N Central Ave and answers at (630) 350-3530. If water is surging from the street side of your meter, that office is the first call.

Sanitary sewer backups run on a separate track. The city asks you to call Public Works at (630) 350-3542 on weekdays between 7:00 a.m. and 3:30 p.m., or the police non-emergency line at (630) 766-2060 after hours. If the city main is blocked, the city clears it at no cost to you. If the main is clear, the service line between the house and the main belongs to the property owner.

The city says that if you hire a plumber before contacting it, you pay the plumber even when the blockage is in the main. So call the city first, then bring us in for extraction and disinfection through our sewage backup cleanup service.

When water is spreading in Wood Dale

Water moving across a basement floor or dripping from a ceiling leaves little time to think. Work through these four moves in order, then call (630) 580-6401 at any hour. Tell the dispatcher where the water started, which rooms are wet, and whether the power is off.

Find the shut-off

Close the fixture valve or the main water valve inside the house. If you cannot find one, or the water is coming from the street, call Wood Dale Public Works and say it is an active leak.

Power to wet rooms

Switch off breakers for wet rooms only if you can reach the panel on dry floor. If water touches outlets or the panel, stay out and call 911 or the electric utility first.

Raise what you can

Lift rugs, electronics, books and cardboard boxes onto counters or shelves. Leave heavy furniture where it is, since dragging it across wet flooring can crack boards and strain your back.

Keep people clear

Keep children and pets out of any water that came from a drain, a toilet or a creek. Photograph the damage before you move anything, and keep receipts for emergency repairs.

Salt Creek and the stream gage at Wood Dale

The USGS runs streamgage 05531175 on Salt Creek at Wood Dale. Its flood-inundation maps cover a 1.6-mile reach that starts upstream of the former Chicago, Milwaukee, St. Paul & Pacific Railroad crossing and ends at Elizabeth Drive. If your street falls inside that reach, check those maps and the FEMA flood map for your address before you decide what to dry and what to discard.

The creek has been tested before. A July 2010 flood peaked at 14.08 feet of gage height, and a September 2008 flood was used to verify the mapping model. Restoration crews treat water that has come out of a creek as contaminated, and standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, so ask your insurer about flood coverage. Our flood damage cleanup crews handle that kind of loss.

What the work looks like on a Wood Dale property

Crews start by finding the source and measuring how far the water went. Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show wet material behind finished walls without cutting every wall open. Then water extraction removes the standing water, which is always the fastest way to limit what soaks upward into drywall.

Drying comes next, and it takes days rather than hours. Air movers push dry air across surfaces while LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room, and readings are logged until wet materials return to dry standards. The process is covered in more detail on our structural drying and dehumidification page.

Ask any restoration company whether its crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500, the water damage restoration standard.

Permits and repairs after the drying is done

Wood Dale lists water heater replacement among its Same Day Permits, alongside roofing, siding, windows, and furnace and air conditioner replacement. The Community Development office is at (630) 766-5133. The city reviews same day permits in one business day and says times can slip during busy periods.

Before anyone replaces a sewer line, the city requires a permit and inspects the line. For drywall or flooring work, ask the city whether a permit applies, and ask your insurer before permanent repairs.

Questions about Wood Dale

Do you cover every ZIP code in Wood Dale?

Wood Dale uses ZIP code 60191, and we cover it from the Bensenville line on the east to the Itasca side on the west, and from the Elk Grove Village border down to Addison. A second code, 60399, appears in some ZIP lists with Bensenville as its alias and no residents, so it does not change coverage.

Is emergency water removal available in Wood Dale?

Yes. Emergency dispatch runs 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays, and we offer same-day response when available. We do not promise an arrival time, but we will tell you what to do while you wait.

Does the city fix a sewer backup inside my house?

The city maintains the main sewer and clears it at no cost if that is where the blockage is. The service line from the house to the main is the owner's responsibility. Cleanup of the wet floor and walls is separate work that we handle.

Can I get a permit quickly for a water heater replacement in Wood Dale?

The city lists water heater replacement as a Same Day Permit with a one business day review. Call Community Development to confirm what applies to your job.

Is creek flooding from Salt Creek covered by homeowners insurance?

Most policies do not cover flood unless flood coverage was bought separately. Sudden and accidental damage inside the home is often covered, so confirm coverage with your insurer.

Do you serve businesses in Wood Dale?

Yes. Our commercial service covers offices, warehouses and multi-tenant buildings, and the same 24/7 line applies.
