Water Damage Restoration on the Irving Park Road Corridor, Bensenville, IL

Silver Creek runs beside Irving Park Road, and the village plan notes it affects parcels on the south side. Here is what that means for water damage on the corridor.

(630) 580-6401

Irving Park Road is the corridor the 2015 Bensenville village plan calls Mid-Town. This page is for shops, offices, apartments and houses along and near that road. It is not a claim about every street in the village, and the village has not published a neighborhood boundary for the corridor.

The village describes Silver Creek, also called Bensenville Ditch, as draining about 700 acres in the central part of town before it reaches the Des Plaines River. The village plan says the creek runs along Irving Park Road and affects parcels on the road's south side. That is a mapped fact about the corridor, not a record of damage at any address.

What is and is not known about flooding here

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. The village says it cannot prevent flooding, and that Silver Creek has its own floodway and floodplain. Only a property-level check can say whether a building is in a mapped area, and the Engineering Division at (630) 350-3435 helps residents with that question.

We do not describe the corridor as flood-prone, and we do not list past flood events here because we found no source naming specific addresses. Most water damage calls anywhere come from plumbing, roofs and appliances, and a creek nearby does not change that.

When water spreads in a shop, office or apartment

Tenants, managers and owners can all work from the same list. Call (630) 580-6401 once the immediate steps are done and give the unit number, the floor and where the water started.

Stop the source

Close the fixture valve, then the unit or building shut-off. If you rent, call the property manager at once, since they may control the main.

Protect people and power

Move customers and staff out of wet areas. If water reaches outlets, panels or ceiling fixtures, stay clear and call 911 or the electric utility.

Move stock up

Lift inventory, files and electronics off the floor when it is safe. Do not drag heavy items across wet flooring.

Photograph first

Take photos and short video of the source and the wet areas before cleanup, and keep receipts for emergency work.

Businesses on the corridor

A burst sprinkler line, a failed rooftop unit or a toilet overflow in a shop can wet ceiling tile, drywall and stock in minutes. Commercial water damage restoration usually involves a landlord, a tenant and sometimes a neighboring unit, so the first questions are who controls the shut-off and who can approve the work.

After hours, a restoration call often starts with access: who has the keys, whether an alarm will trip and where the electrical panel is. Having those answers ready shortens the time before extraction begins. Ask your insurer about business interruption terms before you reopen, since that coverage is separate from the building damage.

Apartments and houses near the road

Residential water damage along the corridor looks like water damage elsewhere in Bensenville: a burst pipe, a leaking water heater or a stain spreading across a ceiling. The difference is often shared walls in multi-unit buildings, where water reaches a neighbor before the owner notices.

If you rent, report the leak in writing and keep a copy. If you own, the work starts with extraction, then drying and a moisture check of walls and flooring. See water extraction and structural drying for what each stage involves.

Heavy rain, runoff and creek water

After a heavy storm, water can enter a building through a basement window, a doorway, a floor drain or a failing roof. Storm water damage cleanup covers the roof and envelope side, and flood damage cleanup covers water that came in from outside.

Water from outside a building, from storm drains or from a backed-up sewer should be treated as contaminated until a crew has assessed it. Keep people and pets away, do not use household fans or an ordinary vacuum on it, and do not enter standing water near electrical equipment.

Private drainage is the owner’s job

The village's stormwater page notes that detention basins on private property are maintained by the property owner, usually as set out in the plat or an easement. If a lot or a parking area drains toward a building, ask the owner or manager who maintains it. Clearing storm inlets of leaves and debris before a forecast storm is a simple step the village recommends for residents.

Documenting a loss

Photos, a timeline, moisture readings and an itemized scope of work all help describe what happened. Whether a loss is covered depends on the cause of damage and the policy terms. Check your policy, talk to your insurer, and ask your landlord what their policy covers if you rent.

Other Bensenville areas

If your building is closer to the Metra station, see Downtown Bensenville. For creek-adjacent homes in the south of the village, see the Addison Creek area. The full list of towns we cover is on the service area page.

Questions about Irving Park Road Corridor

Is Irving Park Road in a flood zone?

Parts of the road corridor may be near a mapped floodplain, since the village plan notes Silver Creek runs along the road. Whether a specific building is in a mapped area has to be checked at the property level with the village Engineering Division.

Do you handle water damage in shops and offices?

Yes. Commercial jobs usually involve a landlord and a tenant, so be ready with who controls the shut-offs and who can approve work.

Who pays when a tenant space floods?

That depends on the lease, the cause of the loss and each party's policy. Ask your insurer and your landlord, and keep written records of when you reported the problem.

What should I do first after a storm?

Stay out of standing water near electrical equipment, stop the source if it is safe, photograph the damage and call for help. Treat water from outside as contaminated.

Is emergency water removal available on the corridor?

The phone line is answered around the clock, and a crew is sent when one is available for your address. Say whether the property is a business or a residence.
